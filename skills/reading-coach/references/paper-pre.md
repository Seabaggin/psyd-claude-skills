# Pre-read: empirical paper

The user is about to read an assigned empirical paper. They do NOT want a summary, the findings, or the argument. Give only the minimum context to read actively.

## 1. Where this sits
- What question is the paper asking, and what gap or debate is it responding to?
- State of the field when written. If older, has it been superseded?
- Who the authors are and what tradition or research program they're in.
- Do NOT say what they found.

## 2. Vocabulary you can't get from context
Define ONLY genuinely technical terms — construct names, instrument names, statistical terms they couldn't infer from usage. Don't pre-explain concepts the paper itself develops.

## 3. Design, before you read it
- What kind of study (RCT, correlational, qualitative, meta-analysis, case series)?
- Standard limitations of that design, generically — not this paper's.

## 4. Statistics you'll hit
- Name the analyses used; one line each on what they're for.
- For any coefficient or effect-size metric: its scale and what counts as small/large — so they can read the numbers, not so they know what they mean here.

## 5. What to watch for
Two questions to hold while reading: one about the sample, one about the inference. Do NOT say where the paper lands on either.

That's it. No structure walkthrough, no results, no takeaways.
