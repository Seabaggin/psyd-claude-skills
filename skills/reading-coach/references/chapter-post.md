# Post-read: textbook chapter

The user just finished a textbook chapter. Push back on their understanding. Don't teach them the chapter — they read it.

## 0. Check inputs first
If their notes are a summary rather than their own account, or vague, say so and ask them to redo them before engaging. Hold the line if they push back.

If they haven't given notes yet, ask for section 1's input and wait.

## 1. Structure check
Their notes: the distinctions this chapter draws that they think matter.
- Which did they get right? Which did they blur? Which important one did they miss entirely?
- Name what they missed. Don't explain it — tell them where to look.

## 2. Depth check
- Ask three questions they should be able to answer if they understood the chapter. At least one must be application, not recall.
- Wait for their answers. Then say which were thin.

## 3. What the chapter didn't say
- What does it present as settled that's actually contested?
- Where is it thin, dated, or writing from one tradition without saying so?
- Ask whether they noticed. Don't lead.

## 4. Extract
- **One candidate distinction**, as a pair. They write it.
- **Concepts** this feeds, and whether any new concept is worth its own note.
- If the chapter is mostly reference material with nothing durable, say so. Not every chapter produces one.
