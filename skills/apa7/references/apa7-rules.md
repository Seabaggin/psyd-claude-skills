# APA 7th Edition Rule Catalog (student papers)

This is the checklist the skill audits against. It is compiled from the APA 7
*student* sample paper's own margin annotations and the Purdue OWL. Default to
**student-paper** rules unless the user says the paper is professional; the two
differ mainly on the title page, running head, and abstract (noted below).

How to use it: go category by category. For each rule, decide **Pass**,
**Fail**, or **Can't verify from this input**. Quote the offending text and give
the fix. Do not invent violations — if the text doesn't show a rule being
broken, it passes.

Severity guide:
- **Error** — breaks an explicit APA rule a grader will mark (wrong heading
  format, missing page number, quote without page number, mis-ordered references).
- **Warning** — likely wrong or inconsistent, but context-dependent (non-standard
  font, a number that might be an allowed exception).
- **Check** — can't be verified from the input alone (needs the source to confirm
  a page number, needs the live document to see spacing) or is a judgment call.

## 1. Whole-document formatting

- **Margins:** 1 inch on all four sides.
- **Font:** legible and consistent throughout. Recommended: Times New Roman 12,
  Calibri 11, Arial 11, Georgia 11, Lucida Sans Unicode 10, or Computer Modern 10.
  One font for the whole paper (headings included).
- **Line spacing:** double-spaced *everywhere* — title page, body, block quotes,
  table notes, and references. No extra space added before/after paragraphs.
- **Alignment:** body text is flush left / ragged right (left-aligned). Do **not**
  justify (do not force straight right edges).
- **Paragraph indent:** first line of every body paragraph indented 0.5". Use the
  paragraph ruler/format, not spaces or a tab where avoidable.
  **Exception: block style.** Some assignments (clinical reports, case write-ups,
  memos) allow or ask for block paragraphs, which have no indent. If every body
  paragraph is unindented the same way, report it once as a 🔎 Check ("block style:
  fine if the assignment allows it"), not as an Error. Flag only *mixed* indenting.
  The blank lines between block paragraphs belong to that style, so don't flag them
  either; when they're used consistently, mention them inside the same Check.
- **Page numbers:** top-right of the header, on every page, starting at 1 on the
  title page. Student papers show **only** the page number in the header.
- **Spacing after end punctuation:** one space after a period.

## 2. Title page (student)

The student title page has no separate cover sheet elements beyond these, all
**centered** and **double-spaced**, positioned in the upper half of page 1
(the sample places the title 3–4 lines below the top margin):

1. **Title** — bold, centered, **title case**. Concise; no "Introduction" wording.
2. One blank (double-spaced) line, then **author name(s)** — written *First
   M. Last*. No titles (Dr.) or degrees (PhD).
   - Two authors on one line joined by **and**: `James P. Bavis and Ahn G. Nu`.
   - (In the *reference list*, by contrast, joint authors use an ampersand.)
3. **Affiliation** — the department and institution responsible for the course,
   e.g., `Department of English, Purdue University`.
4. **Course number and name**, e.g., `ENGL 101: First Year Writing`.
5. **Instructor** name and title, e.g., `Dr. Richard Teeth`.
6. **Assignment due date**, e.g., `January 30, 2020` (month spelled out).

Student papers do **not** include a running head, an author note, an abstract, or
keywords unless the instructor asks. If any appear, flag as *Check* (may be an
instructor requirement) rather than a hard error.

**On page 2 and after:** the full paper title repeats — bold and centered — above
the first body paragraph. There is **no "Introduction" heading**; the opening
section is understood to be the introduction.

## 3. Headings (5 levels)

Headings signal the structure. APA has five levels; most student papers only need
the first three. Use them in order (you can't jump from Level 1 to Level 3).
Common top-level sections (Literature Review, Method, Results, Discussion) are
Level 1. Every heading uses **title case**.

| Level | Format | Text placement |
|------|--------|----------------|
| 1 | **Centered, Bold, Title Case** | New paragraph, indented, below the heading |
| 2 | **Flush Left, Bold, Title Case** | New paragraph, indented, below the heading |
| 3 | ***Flush Left, Bold Italic, Title Case*** | New paragraph, indented, below the heading |
| 4 | Indented, **Bold, Title Case, ending with a period.** | Text begins **on the same line**, same paragraph |
| 5 | Indented, ***Bold Italic, Title Case, ending with a period.*** | Text begins **on the same line**, same paragraph |

Common errors to catch:
- An "Introduction" heading at the top of the body (there shouldn't be one).
- A heading that is italic when it should be bold, or centered when it should be
  flush left (wrong level formatting).
- Sentence case instead of title case.
- Skipping a level (e.g., a Level 3 nested directly under a Level 1).
- Level 4/5 headings written on their own line instead of running into the text.

## 4. In-text citations

- **Paraphrase:** `(Author, Year)` — no page number required, though a page/section
  is encouraged to help the reader locate the idea.
- **Direct quote:** `(Author, Year, p. 91)` or `(pp. 113–121)` — a page number is
  **required**. Each quoted passage gets its own page number, even from the same
  source.
- **Narrative form:** `Theall (2017) argues that…` (author in the sentence, year in
  parentheses right after the name).
- **Two authors:** ampersand inside parentheses `(Remmers & Brandenburg, 1927)`;
  the word **and** in narrative text `Remmers and Brandenburg (1927)`.
- **Three or more authors:** first author + `et al.` — `(Spooren et al., 2017)`.
  Note the period is after **al** (`et al.`), and the sentence/parenthetical
  period comes after `al.` This shortening applies to **in-text only**, never to
  the reference-list entry.
- **Several works in one parenthetical:** list them **alphabetically**, separated
  by **semicolons**: `(Gaff & Simpson, 1994; Lewis, 1996; McKeachie, 1996)`.
- **Same author(s), multiple works:** name once, years separated by commas:
  `(Knoch, 2007, 2009)`.
- **Secondary (indirect) source** — you cite something quoted in another work you
  actually read: `(Seldin, 1993, as cited in Pounder, 2007)`. Put **only the
  source you read** (Pounder) in the reference list.
- **Group/organization author:** spell the name out. You may abbreviate on later
  mentions if it's well known. The reference entry begins with the full org name
  (e.g., `U.S. Department of Education`).
- **Abbreviations:** spell out on first use, then abbreviate — except terms so
  well known they need no expansion (e.g., CIA). Define `SET` the first time as
  `student evaluation of teacher (SET)`.
- **"See, e.g., …"** is the way to point at a few illustrative sources:
  `(see, e.g., Bloom et al., 1956; Cashin, 1990; Theall, 2017)`.

**Cross-check:** every in-text citation must have a matching reference-list entry,
and every reference entry must be cited at least once in the text.

## 5. Direct quotations

- **Short quote (fewer than 40 words):** run into the text in double quotation
  marks, with the citation and page number: `"…is guesswork" (p. 91)`.
- **Block quote (40 words or more):** start on a new line, indent the whole block
  0.5", no quotation marks, still double-spaced. The citation goes **after** the
  closing punctuation. If the author/year isn't already in the lead-in sentence,
  put them in the parenthetical with the page numbers: `(pp. 8–9)`.
- Quotations from different locations in the same source each carry their own page
  number.

## 6. Numbers, statistics & mechanics

- **Words vs. numerals:** spell out zero through nine; use numerals for 10 and up
  (`32 items`, `six topic domains`).
- **Always numerals** regardless of size: percentages, ratios, dates, times, ages,
  sample/population sizes, and specific numbered places in a series.
- **Always words:** any number that **begins a sentence, title, or heading** (or
  reword so it doesn't).
- **Decimal fractions:**
  - Put a **leading zero** before a decimal that *can* exceed 1: `0.34–0.41`.
  - **No leading zero** for a quantity that *cannot* exceed 1 (correlations,
    proportions, and *p* values): `p = .05`, `r = .42`.
- **Number ranges** use an en dash, not a hyphen: `0.34–0.41`, `pp. 113–121`.
- **Serial (Oxford) comma:** include it before the final item in a list of three
  or more.

## 7. Tables & figures

- Numbered sequentially **in order of first mention** (`Table 1`, `Table 2`…;
  `Figure 1`, `Figure 2`…). Tables and figures have **separate** number series —
  a Figure 1 can appear before a Table 1.
- **Number label** (`Table 1`) is **bold**, on its own line.
- **Title** on the next line: *italic, title case*, brief and descriptive.
- Reference them in the text by number (`presented in Table 1`), never by position
  (`the table below`).
- **Notes** go beneath, led by `Note.` (the word *Note* italic). General notes
  first, then specific notes keyed by superscript lowercase letters. Notes are
  optional.
- Design: minimal borders, no shading unless it carries meaning (then explain it
  in the note). Repeat column headers when a table spans pages.

## 8. Reference list

- Starts on a **new page**. Heading `References` (or `Reference` for a single
  source) is **bold and centered** at the top.
- Entries in **alphabetical order** by the first author's surname.
- **Double-spaced**, with a **0.5" hanging indent** on every entry (first line
  flush left, continuation lines indented). Don't fake it with tabs.
- One entry per cited source; every cited source appears once.
- **Author names:** surname first, then initials (`Ambady, N., & Rosenthal, R.`).
  Two authors joined by an **ampersand**. List up to **20** authors; for **21+**,
  give the first 19, an ellipsis `…`, then the final author. (This 20-author rule
  is for the reference list; in-text still uses `et al.`)
- **No date:** use `(n.d.)`.
- **Capitalization:** article, chapter, and book **titles** use **sentence case**
  (capitalize only the first word, the first word after a colon, and proper
  nouns). **Periodical (journal) names** use **title case** and are italicized.
- **DOIs/URLs:** online academic sources require a DOI or stable URL when
  available, formatted `https://doi.org/…`. APA 7 drops "Retrieved from" for most
  sources and drops the publisher **location** for books.

Entry templates:
- **Journal article:**
  `Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title of the article in sentence case.` +
  ` *Journal Name in Title Case, Volume*(Issue), pages. https://doi.org/xxxx`
  (journal name and volume italic; issue number and pages not italic).
- **Book:**
  `Author, A. A. (Year). *Title of the book in sentence case* (2nd ed.). Publisher.`
- **Chapter in an edited book:**
  `Author, A. A. (Year). Title of the chapter. In E. E. Editor (Ed.),` +
  ` *Book title* (pp. 113–121). Publisher.`
- **Group/organization author:**
  `American Association of University Professors. (n.d.). *Title*. URL`

## 9. Appendices

- Begin **after** the reference list, each on a **new page**.
- Label `Appendix` (or `Appendix A`, `Appendix B`… when there is more than one),
  **bold and centered**. If an appendix is a single table or figure, the bold
  centered `Appendix` label replaces the usual table/figure label, and an italic
  title describes it (as with a table/figure title).
- Tables/figures **inside** an appendix take the appendix letter: `Table A1`,
  `Figure A1`. With one unlettered appendix, use `A`.
- Body paragraphs inside an appendix are indented normally, like the main text.

## 10. Student vs. professional differences

If the user says the paper is **professional** (journal submission, not a class
assignment), adjust these:
- **Running head:** required — the title in ALL CAPS, ≤50 characters, flush left in
  the header, with the page number flush right. (Students omit it.)
- **Title page:** adds an **author note** in the bottom half (ORCID iDs, affiliation
  changes, disclosures/acknowledgements, corresponding-author contact). No course,
  instructor, or due-date lines.
- **Abstract:** usually required — its own page after the title page, `Abstract`
  bold and centered, one double-spaced paragraph ≤250 words, optional italic
  `Keywords:` line indented below.

Everything in sections 1 and 3–9 applies to both paper types.
